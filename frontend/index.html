How Lumarium Works

A shared token sequence moves through complementary linguistic and statistical analysis layers.

1. Tokenization

Words, punctuation, numbers, URLs, mentions, and hashtags are identified and normalized.

2. Parallel tagging

Rules, a Hidden Markov Model, and a Transformer produce candidate tags.

3. Arbitration

The arbiter compares candidates and confidence before choosing each final tag.

4. Enrichment

Language, morphology, translation, entities, and WordNet evidence add context.

The Three Tagging Approaches

Each tier contributes a different kind of evidence for code-mixed POS tagging.

Rule-Based Tagger

Uses deterministic lexical, token-shape, and morphological rules for Hindi pronouns, postpositions, particles, light verbs, temporal words, punctuation, numbers, URLs, and fused forms such as dog-ne.

Rule confidence is calibrated from measured training-data precision.

First-Order HMM

Learns tag transitions and token emissions, uses add-k smoothing, and applies suffix evidence to rare or unseen words.

Viterbi decoding selects the sentence sequence; forward-backward probabilities provide token confidence.

HingRoBERTa Transformer (Primary)

A pretrained code-mixed RoBERTa model (l3cube-pune/hing-roberta) fine-tuned for 17 UD tags. Uses contextual subword BPE tokenization tailored for Romanized Hindi-English code-mixing.

Achieves 91.94% test accuracy, 0.8917 Macro-F1, and 85.56% on out-of-vocabulary tokens.

Linguistic Analysis Layers

Supporting analyses help explain the grammatical and linguistic context around each prediction.

Tokenization & Normalization

Recognizes words, punctuation, numbers, URLs, mentions, hashtags, and hyphenated forms. Repeated elongations such as sooooo are normalized before tagging.

Language Identification

Classifies tokens as Hindi, English, or Other and reports language ratios, Code-Mixing Index, dominant language, and switch points.

Morphological Analysis

Uses heuristics to identify possible roots, suffixes, case markers, number, light verbs, and basic tense clues, with English lemmatization where available.

Universal Dependencies POS Tagset

A shared vocabulary lets every model and evaluation label be compared consistently.

ADJ · ADP · ADV · AUX · CCONJ · DET · INTJ · NOUN · NUM · PART · PRON · PROPN · PUNCT · SCONJ · SYM · VERB · X

For example, NOUN identifies naming words, VERB identifies actions, ADP identifies relational words such as mein or ko, and X covers unknown or miscellaneous forms.

Dataset & Evaluation Method

The reported benchmark is the LinCE Hindi-English code-mixed POS dataset. Labels are normalized into the shared UD representation.

  1. Calibrate rules on training data.
  2. Train the HMM on token sequences.
  3. Tune arbiter settings on development data.
  4. Evaluate on the held-out test set.

English POS Validation Layer (WordNet)

Compare translated English tokens with WordNet lexical POS evidence.

WordNet provides supporting lexical evidence for English tokens. It does not replace the ensemble prediction or act as an independent gold-standard annotator.

Limitations

  • The reported benchmark evaluates Hindi-English code-mixed text.
  • Romanized code-mixed spelling can vary significantly.
  • Rule coverage is intentionally partial, so rules may abstain.
  • HMM predictions are less reliable for unknown words.
  • Confidence scores come from different model mechanisms and are not directly equivalent across tiers.
  • WordNet evidence applies primarily to translated English tokens.
  • Performance varies across POS categories and ambiguous word forms.
